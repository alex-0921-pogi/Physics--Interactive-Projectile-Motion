Kinematics & Projectile Motion Decomposition

Projectile motion is a form of motion experienced by an object that is thrown near the Earth's surface and moves along a curved path under the action of gravity only. Key to understanding it is the independence of horizontal and vertical motions.

Horizontal Component ($x$-axis)

Assuming negligible air resistance, acceleration in the horizontal direction is zero ($a_x = 0$). Velocity remains constant.

$v_{0x} = v_0 \cdot \cos(\theta)$
$x(t) = v_{0x} \cdot t$
$a_x = 0$
Vertical Component ($y$-axis)

Subject to constant downward gravitational acceleration ($a_y = -g$). Vertical velocity decreases going up, becomes 0 at peak, and increases downward.

$v_{0y} = v_0 \cdot \sin(\theta)$
$y(t) = y_0 + v_{0y} \cdot t - \frac{1}{2}g t^2$
$v_y(t) = v_{0y} - g \cdot t$

Essential Derived Equations (Flat Ground $y_0 = 0$)

Time of Flight ($T$)
$$T = \frac{2 v_0 \sin\theta}{g}$$

Total time the projectile stays airborne before returning to original height.

Maximum Height ($H$)
$$H = \frac{v_0^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g}$$

Peak vertical altitude achieved when vertical velocity component drops to zero.

Horizontal Range ($R$)
$$R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}$$

Maximum horizontal distance traveled. Peak range occurs at $\theta = 45^\circ$.

Concept #1

What angle yields the maximum horizontal range on flat ground?

Click to flip
Explanation

45 Degrees.
The range formula is $R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}$. Since $\sin(\theta)$ reaches its maximum value of $1$ at $90^\circ$, setting $2\theta = 90^\circ$ gives $\theta = 45^\circ$.
